## What is *not* restricted

The noncommercial condition is about **use of zen-ui**, not about your source
being open or your project being free. You may read the code, fork it, modify
it, run it, and redistribute your changes for any permitted purpose. Reading
the source to learn from it is always fine. So is quoting it in a talk, a blog
post, or a bug report.

You also keep whatever fair-use rights the law gives you. The license says so
in as many words, and it does not try to narrow them.

## Asking for a commercial license

Email **pillai.rajesh@gmail.com** with:

1. Who you are — company or individual, and roughly how big.
2. What you want to build with it.
3. Which bindings you need (`react`, `solid`, `vanilla`, `web-components`).

There is no form and no minimum. Small teams, single products, and internal
tools are all straightforward. The point of the license is to know who is
building on this and to keep the door open for the cases that should be paid,
not to make the common case difficult.

## Why this license and not an open source one

zen-ui is **source-available**, not open source. That distinction is
deliberate and worth stating plainly rather than blurring: the Open Source
Definition forbids restricting the field of use, so a license with a
noncommercial condition is not an open source license no matter how freely the
source is published. Anyone describing zen-ui as open source — including its
own documentation — is getting it wrong.

PolyForm Noncommercial was chosen over a hand-written "personal use only"
notice because it is drafted by lawyers, written in plain English, has an SPDX
identifier (`PolyForm-Noncommercial-1.0.0`) that license scanners recognise,
and defines the terms that would otherwise be argued about — what a permitted
purpose is, what happens to modifications, and what happens on a first
violation (32 days to come into compliance, rather than an immediate
termination nobody intended).

## Contributing

Contributions are welcome, with one thing to be aware of. Algorisys Technologies
can grant commercial licenses because it holds the copyright in the whole work;
a contributor keeps copyright in their own lines unless they say otherwise. So
by opening a pull request you agree that your contribution is licensed to the
project under the same terms as the rest of it, and that Algorisys Technologies
may include it in commercial licenses granted to others.

If that does not work for you, open an issue describing the change instead of a
pull request — a well-described bug is worth as much and carries none of this.
