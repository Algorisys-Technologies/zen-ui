# 13.1.0

*2026-11-18*

**Keep track of the date while you scroll a schedule.** Gantt and
ProductionSchedule can show a second header row naming the date, month or year
above the columns. It stays in view as you scroll sideways, so you can follow an
order across weeks without looking up at the toolbar to know which month you
are in.

Nothing breaks. The new row is off until you turn it on, and a chart without it
looks exactly as before.

## New

- **A date row above the timeline.** Turn on `headerGroups` and the header gets
  a second row:
  - the **date above the hours** in a Day view ("Tue 21 Jul 2026"),
  - the **month above the days** in Week, Month and Fit views, so a week that
    runs from 29 June to 5 July reads "June 2026 | July 2026",
  - the **month above the weeks** in a Quarter view,
  - the **year above the months** in a Year view.

  ```tsx
  <Gantt tasks={plan} headerGroups />
  <ProductionSchedule resources={cells} operations={jobs} headerGroups />
  ```

  In web components it is an attribute:

  ```html
  <zen-gantt header-groups></zen-gantt>
  ```

  Each label sticks just past the frozen left-hand pane, so when you scroll
  sideways through a long plan, the month or date of the columns you are looking
  at stays on screen. Where a group is too narrow for "June 2026" it shows
  "Jun" rather than cutting the name off.

  With the row on, the small hint that used to repeat the month or year under
  the first column of each new period is dropped, because the row above now
  says it once.

## Fixed

- **The Day view was wrong on the two days a year the clocks change.** On the
  spring change it showed a 24th hour that really belonged to the next day, and
  every hour after the change was labelled an hour off. It now follows the
  clock on the wall: 23 hours on the day the clocks go forward, 25 on the day
  they go back, each labelled with the hour it really starts at.
